科学史上最荒诞一幕：百名学者看见了不存在的光

虎嗅 (AI原生100等)

The article recounts the 'N-ray' episode of 1903-1906, when roughly 100 researchers, including respected French physicist René Blondlot, published over 300 papers on a nonexistent radiation. The illusion stemmed from experiments that relied on the naked eye judging near-threshold light in near-total darkness, where expectation shaped perception; American physicist Robert W. Wood exposed it by secretly removing the aluminum prism, yet observers still reported the same spectral lines. The case illustrates how even trained scientists can collectively self-deceive, and why rigorous methodology remains science's best defense against its own illusions.

1903到1906年间，约100名学者发表了300多篇论文，研究一种根本不存在的“N射线”，领头人还是法国颇受尊敬的物理学家布朗德洛特。问题出在实验靠肉眼在近乎全黑的环境里判断微弱光斑的明暗，预期悄悄左右了感知；美国物理学家伍德趁黑偷走铝棱镜，对方却照样报出“谱线”，闹剧就此收场。这个故事提醒我们：科学最可靠的防线，恰恰是它不断怀疑自己的能力。

  • About 100 scientists and doctors published over 300 papers on N-rays between 1903 and 1906, despite the phenomenon being nonexistent.
  • The experiments used the human eye as the primary detector, judging near-threshold light in near-total darkness, making them highly susceptible to expectation bias.
  • Robert W. Wood secretly removed the aluminum prism, yet Blondlot and his assistant still reported the same spectral lines, exposing the illusion.
💡 Why it matters: For AI practitioners, the N-ray affair is a cautionary tale about confirmation bias and weak ground truth: when evaluation depends on subjective human judgment, even experts can 'see' patterns that aren't there, underscoring the need for blinded, reproducible benchmarks.
🇨🇳 对AI从业者来说，N射线事件是确认偏误和“弱真值”的经典警示：当评估依赖主观判断时，连专家也会“看见”不存在的模式，这正说明盲测和可复现基准有多重要。
