月球上用什么时间？

虎嗅 (AI原生100等)

As lunar bases and resource development plans accelerate, a dedicated lunar time standard becomes urgent because clocks on the Moon run about 58 microseconds per day faster than on Earth due to weaker gravity, making direct use of UTC impractical for high-precision navigation. At the 28th General Conference on Weights and Measures in October 2026, resolutions were passed to end the leap second mechanism by 2027 and to define an International Lunar Reference Time Scale traceable to UTC. Among three proposed approaches—copying Earth time, establishing a centralized lunar timekeeping center, or decentralized timekeeping—a Chinese research team has pioneered the decentralized model, which avoids single points of failure and aligns with the relativistic principle of equivalence.

随着月球基地和资源开发计划加速推进，月球专属时间标准变得愈发迫切。由于月球引力仅为地球的约六分之一，月球上的时钟每天比地球快约58微秒，直接沿用协调世界时会导致导航定位误差累积到十几千米。2026年10月第28届国际计量大会通过决议，将于2027年5月正式终结闰秒机制，并为建立国际月球参考时标迈出关键一步。在三种月球时间方案中，中国研究团队率先提出的去中心化守时方案备受关注，它不依赖单一中心节点，从根本上消除了单点故障风险。

  • Lunar clocks run about 58 microseconds per day faster than Earth clocks due to weaker gravity, and this offset fluctuates, making simple correction impossible.
  • The 28th CGPM in October 2026 resolved to end the leap second mechanism by May 2027 and to define an International Lunar Reference Time Scale traceable to UTC.
  • Three approaches exist: copying Earth time, a centralized lunar timekeeping center (like ESA's NovaMoon plan), or decentralized timekeeping.
💡 Why it matters: Developers and AI practitioners working on space systems, satellite navigation, and distributed time synchronization will need to account for relativistic effects and design fault-tolerant, decentralized timekeeping architectures for future multi-planetary infrastructure.
🇨🇳 对于从事航天系统、卫星导航和分布式时间同步的开发者与AI从业者而言，未来多行星基础设施需要充分考虑相对论效应，并设计容错的去中心化守时架构。

Peking University Guanghua professor Chen Yuyu argues that cheap knowledge—whether from developed countries or AI—does not automatically translate into economic growth, because the missing link is 'organizational technology': the slow, collective process by which a group of people learns to use knowledge together. He introduces the concept of 'two-sided calibration,' where workers and managers form mutually adapted work rules through shared practice, and notes that while production capacity can vanish within years even when no scientific knowledge is lost, rebuilding it takes far longer. The article suggests AI's growth impact depends on whether it changes knowledge supply, organizational calibration, or both, and that industries with fast versus slow calibration will follow very different productivity paths.

北大光华教授陈玉宇抛出一个扎心的问题：发达国家早就是发展中国家的“AI”了，知识一直很便宜，可为什么一百年来真正追上来的国家还是少数？他认为，知道怎么做和一群人真能做成之间，隔着一层“组织技术”——一群人通过共同实践形成相互适配的工作规则，他称之为“双侧校准”。这种能力可以几年内散掉，重建却慢得多，而且知识一页没少。放到AI时代，技术能不能变成增长，得看它改变的是知识供给，还是组织校准，还是两者都改。

  • Cheap knowledge has historically failed to produce growth automatically; most income gaps are attributed to total factor productivity, not capital or schooling.
  • Organizational technology—the collective ability to coordinate knowledge, people, and resources—is not transferable via documents and must be built through practice.
  • 'Two-sided calibration' describes how different roles form mutually adapted work rules; individual experience can be carried away, but shared calibration cannot.
