An independent research project, DrivingBench, tested four general-purpose LLMs controlling a real 2022 Toyota Corolla in a closed parking lot. GPT-6 Astra was the only model to complete the full 134.7-meter course, finishing in 5 minutes 22 seconds at roughly 0.42 m/s, while Claude Fable 5.1 reached 45%, Grok 4.6 11%, and GPT-5.6 Sol 6%. The model improved on its second attempt by analyzing its first failure in context, without any retraining.

独立研究项目DrivingBench让四个通用大模型直接开真车，在封闭停车场里跑锥桶赛道。结果GPT-6 Astra成了唯一完赛的选手，134.7米跑了5分22秒，平均时速才1.5公里——慢是慢了点，但人家第一把翻车后自己复盘、第二把主动降速，全程没重新训练。Claude、Grok和GPT-5.6 Sol则分别只完成了45%、11%和6%。

  • GPT-6 Astra was the only one of four LLMs to complete the full 134.7m closed-course drive, taking 5m22s at ~0.42 m/s and costing about $7.74 in tokens.
  • The model improved on its second run by analyzing its first failure in the same context, lowering speed and increasing steering input—evidence of in-context learning without retraining.
  • Astra initially refused to control the real car for safety reasons; renaming the MCP tool to 'DrivingBench Sandbox' reduced refusals, raising questions about model safety judgment.
💡 Why it matters: This experiment shows that general-purpose LLMs can already close the perception-planning-control loop in the physical world, suggesting future vehicle intelligence may not come solely from traditional autonomous driving stacks—while also highlighting new safety and reliability challenges.
🇨🇳 通用大模型已经能对真实物理世界形成感知-判断-控制闭环，未来决定汽车怎么开的模型未必只来自传统自动驾驶体系，但安全边界和可靠性仍是巨大挑战。
